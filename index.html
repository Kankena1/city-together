    Playtest guide · Rules

    V2 balance test: Just surviving without investing no longer counts as a win. You must actually grow the city. Values are experimental and may require playtesting.

    Goal: By the end of round 18, stay solvent AND reach at least 100 population with 3 completed housing upgrades. If you meet all three goals, choose to continue for a high score; otherwise your city does not achieve the development goal. All players win or lose together.

    Each round: (1) Active player rolls two dice and moves the one shared token; (2) the team resolves the space (buy/decline, draw an event, or special space); (3) the team chooses ONE city action: develop any owned property, repair an ongoing crisis, or save; (4) collect population taxes and pay property maintenance, city operating costs, and crisis costs. Then the next player rolls.

    Economic rules: Start with $1,200 and 40 population. Each resident generates $2 tax per round. Each property has a printed purchase cost; recurring maintenance is the greater of $10 or 10% of its cost (rounded to the nearest $5). Buying a property does not automatically increase population. One housing upgrade costs the greater of $50 or 50% of the property's purchase price (rounded to the nearest $10) and adds 20 residents; max 3 upgrades per property. City operating expenses start at $80 per round and increase by $40 every 3 rounds (rounds 4, 7, 10, etc.). A passive city cannot satisfy the housing upgrade victory goal. Track income versus upcoming bills before spending.

    Spaces: GO gives +$100 each time it is passed or landed on; a tax space charges a one-time fee; Free Parking gives +$40; Jail/Go to Jail charges a one-time $40 administration fee (no skipped turns); railroads and utilities are purchasable property; Chance and Community Chest draw city events. A roll of doubles grants no extra turn. No rent, auctions, trading, mortgages, or color-set monopolies in this first prototype.

    Crises: Event cards can offer a choice between an immediate payment and a recurring penalty. Use the Repair action to remove a recurring penalty by paying its stated repair cost. Loans are not included in version 1. If cash goes below zero after settlement, the city is bankrupt. Purchases and upgrades cannot be paid for with money you do not have.

    Playtest: Is a management action interesting? Is buying property attractive but risky? Does everyone participate? Record the round reached, cash balance, and discussions. All amounts are prototype values, not final balancing.
